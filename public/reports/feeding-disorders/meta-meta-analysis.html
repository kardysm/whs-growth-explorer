A meta-analysis of meta-analyses: retention of added energy

A second-order summary of the three lever subgroups of the steering meta-analysis — 14 fixed-volume, 7 free-volume and 1 portion studies, the same 22 rows. It is not a synthesis of independent meta-analyses.

Version 1.0 · 22 November 2026 · data: feeding-disorders project analyses v1.2.0

Three lever subgroups pool to 45.7% (95% CI 20.7–70.6) retention, I² 68.5%, τ² 80.2. The whole-day variant gives 39.0% (16.0–62.1); without the single portion trial the other two subgroups give 52.2% (35.1–69.4). 1. What is being combined

The three inputs are exactly the lever subgroups in results_steering.json: 14 fixed-volume, 7 free-volume and 1 portion study (14+7+1 = 22 rows). The steering meta-analysis (22 studies, 51.4%) is the study-level pool of those same rows; it is not an independent component and is not excluded for having a different study set. Appetite stimulants and tube weaning are outside the pool because their units differ.

  • Fixed-volume density: COMPX, k=14, retention 51.3% (37.7–64.8).
  • Free-volume density: ED, k=7, retention 54.1% (32.7–75.6).
  • Portion size: one five-day trial, 36.0% (28.2–43.8); it carries 41.4% of the pool weight.
  • Whole-day retention: k=3, 32.9% (14.3–51.5); used in sensitivity because it shares studies with free-volume density.

Appetite stimulants (kg) and tube weaning (proportion) are not pooled because their units differ. The steering meta-analysis is not an additional component: it is the same 22-study pool.

2. Second-order result

The three subgroups give 45.7% (95% CI 20.7–70.6), I² 68.5%. The whole-day variant gives 39.0% (16.0–62.1).

Without the single portion trial, the other two subgroups give 52.2% (35.1–69.4), I² 0.0%. The portion trial carries 41.4% of the weight; the drop from 51.3% to 45.7% comes from that one trial.

3. What this changes

The 45.7% estimate is 5.6 points below the COMPX summary (51.3%) and 5.7 points below the steering pool (51.4%); the intervals overlap. Without the portion trial the two subgroups give 52.2% (35.1–69.4), so the difference is not a new, more precise effect: it is the sensitivity of the average to one small trial with high weight. The direction — about half of added energy remains — holds.

The prediction interval (-93.5% to 184.8%) spans zero and values above 100%. In a new setting, full compensation and full retention are both compatible with the evidence; planning must rest on measurement, not the mean.

4. Limitations

  • Three subgroups are not three independent populations; COMPX and ED are mostly healthy children, and portion is one preschool trial.
  • The summaries already carry uncertainty; their variances do not include uncertainty in τ² or model choice.
  • There are no individual data and no shared mechanism; the result is descriptive, not causal.
  • No Egger test was used: k=3 at the second order.
